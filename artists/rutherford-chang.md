## The project, in the artist's own words

This is the description as published on sovrn.art. It is the artist's text and
is the wording we would ask you to follow where you paraphrase.

> CENTS examines value through the lens of the copper penny. After over a
> century in circulation the lowly denomination has become so commonplace that
> it is considered a worthless nuisance. Yet a penny from any given year will
> always be worth exactly 1¢, according to the United States Treasury.
> Furthermore, those minted in 1982 and before were struck in copper and now
> have a metal value of approximately 3.1¢. Recognizing these discrepancies in
> value, Rutherford Chang removed 10,000 copper cents from circulation and
> archivally documented them.
>
> With approximately 288 billion Lincoln pennies in existence, the portrait of
> Abraham Lincoln on the cent's obverse is the most reproduced piece of art on
> Earth. The course of history has endowed each of these metal discs with unique
> characteristics, which are now encoded as digital artifacts. Images of these
> 10,000 individuals are inscribed as ordinals on 10,000 satoshis, the smallest
> units of Bitcoin, while the physical coins are smelted and cast into a solid
> 68.56-pound copper block.
>
> This metal block is rendered as a three-dimensional digital model and in turn
> inscribed as a 4-megabyte ordinal, comprising the entirety of Bitcoin block
> #839969. Equivalent to casting a copper ingot so heavy and dense that it is
> indestructible, inscribing this data on the fundamental structure of the
> blockchain forms an immutable digital monument. As the traditional penny fades
> into irrelevance and its production is ultimately terminated, its value can
> continue to be redefined through this digitally scarce set of 10,000 and the
> block that encapsulates their transformation.

Two points from that text tend to land hardest with visitors, and are worth
keeping if you shorten:

- The **288 billion** figure, and that Lincoln's portrait is consequently the
  most reproduced piece of art on Earth.
- That the coins were **smelted**. The physical cents no longer exist as coins.
  The irreversibility is the work, not a by-product of it.

## Three numbers worth tightening

**1. The block weighs 68.56 pounds.** Your page says 31 kilograms, which is
correct to the nearest kilogram (68.56 lb = 31.10 kg) but rounds away the
artist's own figure. 68.56 lb is what Chang's text gives and what sovrn.art says
throughout as of 26 August 2026.

You will find other numbers in circulation, and they are the same block rather
than a disagreement:

| source | figure |
|---|---|
| Chang's project description | 68.56 lb |
| UCCA exhibition text | 31 kg |
| Philip Tinari, in Taschen's *On NFTs* | 68 lb |
| sovrn.art before 26 Aug 2026 | 69 lb |

**2. The penny was discontinued after the work was made.** The U.S. Mint
permanently suspended penny production in **November 2025**, which turns the
block into a monument to a coin that is no longer struck. Chang's text
anticipates this — "as the traditional penny fades into irrelevance and its
production is ultimately terminated" — and it happened. Worth a line.

**3. "Melted" vs "smelted".** Chang's text says smelted and cast. A small thing,
but the distinction is doing work: the coins were reduced and recast as a single
object, not simply liquefied.

## Traits — what they are, and what they are not

This matters on a marketplace page more than anywhere else, because the format
invites the assumption that traits work the way they do in a generative
collection. They do not.

- CENTS traits are **not algorithmic**. Nothing was randomly assigned. Every
  trait is a real physical feature of a real coin — wear, strike, discolouration,
  the particular way it left circulation.
- They were **catalogued by the community**, over roughly two years, through
  repeated reviews of the full 10,000, voting rounds and case-by-case discussion
  of thresholds, by a group called the **Trait Council**.
- They are therefore **interpretive and community-authored**, not official from
  the artist.

If you display traits, please attribute them to the Trait Council rather than
presenting them as intrinsic metadata.

- Trait Atlas — every trait, how it forms and how it was catalogued:
  <https://cents.vision/traits-atlas.html>
- Trait Explorer — search and filter all 10,000 by trait, mint year and
  combination: <https://cents.vision/>

## Exhibitions and press of record

**Rutherford Chang: Hundreds and Thousands** — UCCA Center for Contemporary Art,
Beijing, 17 January – 12 April 2026. The first institutional and most
comprehensive solo exhibition of Chang's work, surveying two decades of practice.
Posthumous. Co-curated by **Philip Tinari** (UCCA Director), the artist **Aki
Sasamoto** and **Danielle Chang**, presented in collaboration with the Estate of
Rutherford Chang.

At UCCA, CENTS was shown as the copper block with the 10,000 coins explorable on
touchscreens and in a book. It was set alongside *We Buy White Albums*
(2006–2025), *Game Boy Tetris* (2013–2018), *Class of 2008* (2008–2012), *NBC
Nightly News* (2004), *The Epic* (2004–2005) and *Portraits by Tone* (2005).
